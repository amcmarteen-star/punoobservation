Before you start

What is a monitoring report?

It records how many seedlings are still alive at a reforestation site.

The system turns your counts into a survival rate. Your CENRO then reviews it.

Why can't I find my site in the list?

Pick the municipality first, then the barangay. The site list fills in after that.

You only see sites in your CENRO's area.

Still missing? Ask your CENRO administrator to check the site's record.

It says the site has no planting record. What do I do?

Survival is measured against the number of seedlings planted. Without that number, there is nothing to compare your count to.

Ask an administrator to add the site's planting record. Or choose another site.

Can I pick a future monitoring date?

No. Use the day you did the count. The date cannot be later than today.

Counting

How do I count the seedlings?

Count only seedlings that are alive. The method depends on the site's size:

  • Under {{ full_below }} ha: count every live seedling on the whole site. You can count it in parts, with one row per part.
  • {{ full_below }} ha and over: lay out plots of {{ plot_side }} m × {{ plot_side }} m until they cover {{ target_pct }}% of the site. Enter one row per plot.

After you choose a site, the count plan shows which method applies and how many plots you need.

Do I type the survival rate?

No. The system calculates it from your counts while you type.

  • Whole-site count: live seedlings ÷ seedlings planted.
  • Plots: the average per plot is scaled up to the whole site, then divided by seedlings planted.

The number you see on screen is the number that gets saved.

What does the {{ threshold }}% mean?

It is the DENR reference threshold for survival. The calculation shows whether the site reaches it.

Where should I put the plots?

Spread them across the whole site.

Plots placed only in healthy areas, or only in poor ones, give a wrong picture of the site.

I counted fewer plots than needed. Can I still submit?

Yes. You will see a warning, and the reviewer sees it too.

Fewer plots make the estimate less reliable. Try to reach the full number when you can.

Photos

Which photos do I need?
  • Plot photographs: proof of what you counted. One per plot is ideal.
  • Corner photographs: stand at each corner of the site and take one photo. You need at least 4 corners to draw the boundary.

Use a GPS camera app. The system reads the location and time from each photo, so you never type coordinates.

Will my photo be rejected if it has no GPS?

No. Nothing is rejected automatically.

The photo is accepted but marked for the reviewer to check. A photo is marked when:

  • it has no GPS location or no date
  • it was taken outside the site's barangay
  • it was taken more than 60 days before the monitoring date, or after it
  • the same photo was already used, in this report or an earlier one
Why did my photo lose its location?

Messaging apps like Messenger and Viber often remove the location when they send a photo.

Upload the original photo from your phone's gallery instead.

How big can a photo be?

Up to {{ app_limits.maxFileMb }} MB each. JPG, PNG and HEIC photos work.

Why is the drawn boundary bigger than the real site?

The boundary is the smallest simple shape around all your corner points, like a rubber band around them.

An L-shaped site, or one that bends around a stream, gets its gap filled in. So the drawn area comes out larger.

The system compares the drawn area with the contracted area and warns when they differ by more than 10%.

After you submit

What happens after I submit?

The report is marked Pending and your CENRO administrator is notified.

They approve or reject it, and you get a notification either way.

You can follow it on the My Reports page.

My report was rejected. What now?

Open it in My Reports. The reviewer must give a reason when rejecting, and it is shown there along with any photo warnings.

A submitted report cannot be edited. Fix the problem and submit a new report.

What happens when my report is approved?

The site's survival rate is updated to your report's figure.

If your corner photos made a boundary, it goes to the province for review before it appears on the map.
