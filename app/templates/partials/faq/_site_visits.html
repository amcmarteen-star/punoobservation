What is a site visit?

A field check of a site made from a resident's request. It is for new sites, where there are no seedlings to count yet.

Once seedlings are planted and growing, survival is recorded with a monitoring report instead.

How does an officer get sent to a site?

The CENRO admin assigns them when creating the temporary site. They can assign again from the site's page for each later visit.

The officer gets a notification and an email, and sees the visit under Assigned to you.

Which type of visit should I choose?
  • Pre-planting check: look at the land and walk its corners for the boundary.
  • Planting confirmed: the seedlings are in. Enter how many were actually planted.

A site can have as many visits as needed.

How is the boundary made?

Take one photo at each corner of the site with a GPS camera app. At least 4 corners are needed.

Once the CENRO approves the visit, the boundary goes to the provincial administrator. The site appears on the public map when they publish it.

Why does "seedlings actually planted" matter?

Survival is survivors divided by seedlings planted. Until this number is recorded, monitoring reports for the site show survival as "Unknown".

What happens if the site is not suitable?

Say why in the remarks. When the CENRO approves the visit, they suggest a new planting site.

The requester gets the suggestion by notification and email. The site is marked Not Suitable and the request is rejected.

Who can record and approve visits?

Only an officer with an open assignment records a visit. An administrator of the site's CENRO approves or rejects it.

Approving a Suitable visit also approves the request.

A decision is final. If a visit is rejected, the assignment opens again so the officer can record a new one.
