Using the map

How do I find a site?

Click a municipality to see its sites. Click a barangay to see the sites in it.

On a site card:

  • Locate zooms the map to the site.
  • History shows its past survival counts.
  • The Google Maps button opens it in Google Maps.
What are Area and District at the top?

They filter the map to part of the province.

  • None is the default. It shows no colour or borders, so only the map and the sites show. Towns can still be clicked.
  • Picking an area shows its districts in one colour.
  • Picking a district shows it in its own colour.
How do I switch to satellite view or show barangay outlines?

Open Layers. There you can switch the base map and turn sites, boundaries, outlines and names on or off.

What does the heat map show?

Where activity is concentrated. Choose what to measure: number of sites, seedlings planted, target seedlings or hectares.

Show as picks how it is drawn. Site areas (the default) draws one shape per site. A solid outline is the site's surveyed boundary. A dashed outline is a synthetic approximation for visualization: it has the site's real size in hectares, but its shape and position inside the barangay are made up, because the DENR records give no coordinates. Whole barangays shades each barangay by its total instead.

Is a site's tree at its real location?

Each tree is one site. Sites imported from DENR records have no exact location, so each stands in an approximate area: the site's real size in hectares, at a made-up place inside its barangay. Its tooltip says "Approximate position".

A site moves to its real place once the province publishes its GPS boundary.

Site details

What is Monitoring History?

Past survival counts for a site, oldest to newest, with photos.

Only reports a CENRO has approved are shown.

What do the different site boundaries mean?

Solid blue: an official boundary, measured with GPS and published by the province.

Dashed blue: a proposed site from a request, not surveyed yet. Only staff see these.

Dotted green: an approximate area. It has the site's real size, but its shape and place are made up for the map. No two sites' areas overlap.

Where do the recommended species come from?

From the same recommender as the Species Recommendation page. It compares the barangay's conditions with each species' published growing conditions.

Requests

How do I request a site from the map?

Choose a request type, then use the map tools:

  • Drop pin: click once where the site is.
  • Draw plot: click each corner to measure the area.

Then press Continue to form to finish the request.
