Reading the report

How was the survival rate calculated?
  • Whole site counted: live seedlings ÷ seedlings planted.
  • Sampling plots: the average per plot is scaled up to the whole site, then divided by seedlings planted.

Every step is shown under Survival calculation, so it can be checked by hand.

What does the {{ threshold }}% line mean?

It is the DENR reference threshold for survival. The badge says whether this report reaches it.

What do the amber bars mean?

Plots with fewer than half the average count. Many amber bars mean survival is uneven across the site.

Why is the captured boundary bigger than the contracted area?

The boundary is the smallest simple shape around the corner photos. An L-shaped site, or one that bends around a stream, gets its gap filled in, so it measures larger.

What do the photo labels mean?
  • Inside barangay: the photo's GPS location is inside the site's barangay.
  • No GPS data: the photo has no location. It may have been sent through a messaging app.
  • Flags: something to check, such as a photo taken outside the barangay, too long before the monitoring date, or used before.

Making a decision

What should I check before approving?
  • The counts and notes look believable.
  • Enough plots were counted for the site's size.
  • Flagged photos: open them and decide if they are acceptable.
  • The captured area is close to the contracted area.
What happens when I approve?

The report's survival rate and monitoring date are saved to the site's planting record right away.

If the report has a captured boundary, it goes to the provincial administrator for publication. It does not appear on the map until they publish it.

Do I have to give a reason to reject?

Yes. The field officer sees your note, so say clearly what to fix.

Can I change my decision later?

Yes. Setting a new status replaces the old one, and the officer is notified again.
