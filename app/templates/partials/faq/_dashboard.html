The basics

What does the dashboard show?

Three sections, top to bottom:

  • What has been done: sites and hectares placed under contract, by year and municipality.
  • How well was it done: how much was planted against the target, and how many seedlings survived.
  • What should be done next: well-suited barangays that have no site yet.
Why do I only see some municipalities?

A CENRO account sees only its own CENRO's area. Province-wide accounts see all of Pangasinan.

Why do the charts take a few seconds to load?

Each time you open the page, the system scores every barangay to find the best places to plant next. That takes a moment.

How up to date are the numbers?

They are worked out fresh every time you open the page. Reload it to see the latest.

Reading the charts

What is the delivery rate?

Seedlings actually planted, as a percentage of the contracted target.

Because it is a percentage, a small municipality that planted everything ranks above a big one that fell short.

Where does the survival rate come from?

From each site's planting record. When a CENRO approves a monitoring report, that report's survival rate is saved to the site.

The chart shows the average for each municipality. The dashed line marks the DENR {{ threshold }}% threshold.

Why is a municipality missing from the survival chart?

None of its sites has a survival rate recorded yet.

What is cost per hectare?

The three-year project cost divided by the contracted area. Big differences can come from terrain, species or how hard the site is to reach.

How is "Where to plant next" worked out?

Every barangay gets a suitability score from the species recommender.

"Highly suitable" means the top quarter of barangays by that score. The chart counts the highly suitable barangays that have no reforestation site yet.

What does "Most recommended species" mean?

How many barangays have that species in their top 5. A species high on this list suits many places.
