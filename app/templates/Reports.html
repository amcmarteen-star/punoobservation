How to do this. Count only the seedlings that are alive. How you count depends on the size of the site:

Please read. This estimate assumes seedlings are distributed evenly across the site. Plots placed only in healthy areas, or only in poor ones, will not represent the site. Spread them out.

The result is an estimate from a sample, not a full count. Fewer plots than the plan, or wide variation between plots, makes it less reliable, and the calculation will say so.
Please read. Count every part of the site exactly once. A part counted twice, or missed, changes the survival rate directly.
3

Photographs

Use a GPS camera app. The system reads the location and time out of each photo automatically. You do not type coordinates.

If a photo has no GPS data, or was taken far from the site, it is still accepted — it is marked for the reviewing officer to check. Nothing is rejected automatically.

Evidence of what you counted. One per plot is ideal.

Stand at each corner of the site and take one photo. At least 4 corners are needed to draw the boundary. The system joins the coordinates into a polygon and compares its area against the contracted area.

The boundary is drawn as the smallest shape enclosing all your corner points. If the site is not a simple convex shape — an L-shape, or one bending around a stream — the drawn area will be larger than the real parcel. The comparison against contracted area will show this.
